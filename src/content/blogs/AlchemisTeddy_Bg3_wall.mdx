After experimenting with a **stencil-based see-through effect** 
[Unity Tips – Creating a Stencil See-Through Effect in Unity 6](), 
I quickly noticed limitations. In scenarios such as **tight tunnels** or **parallel wall intersections**, the stencil solution introduced artifacts and did not feel robust.

For a second attempt, I drew inspiration from **Baldur’s Gate III**'s beautiful occlusion cutout system.

<Video href="https://www.youtube.com/embed/SCO6ETXpc78?si=MrJHK_jB8NS828X3&autoplay=1&autohide=1&mute=1&modestbranding=1&playsinline=1&loop=1&playlist=SCO6ETXpc78"></Video>

Luckily, Mojang’s Senior Technical Artist **Brendan “Sully” Sullivan** had already broken down the technique in Unreal Engine 
[80.lv article](https://80.lv/articles/artist-recreated-baldur-s-gate-3-occlusion-cutout-effect), 
which served as a strong reference.

The challenge was now clear: **How do we reproduce this effect inside Unity?**
